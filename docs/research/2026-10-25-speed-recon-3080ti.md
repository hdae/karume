# Speed-up recon on the RTX 3080 Ti, awaiting the 5070 Ti answer check

Question (the maintainer, 2026-10-24): the 5070 Ti and its Chrome cannot be used for now — investigate speed-ups with what
this machine can do (its Chromium is fine; the 5070 Ti then becomes the answer check). Include the perf-ledger items,
anything else noticed, and whether the frequently used existing kernels themselves can be improved.

## 1. Summary

- **Chrome itself is not why the online attention kernel loses on the 5070 Ti.** On the same RTX 3080 Ti, Chromium 156
  (Linux, Vulkan) and Node agree within 1% on both attention routes (§3). What remains is the D3D12 path or Blackwell.
  The leading hypothesis is an occupancy cliff at 128 registers, not decided without the 5070 Ti.
- **The two biggest kernels both have bit-identical rewrites of about ×1.3–1.5 on this GPU**, in forms that keep the
  `gemm.ts:823` MUST (no dynamic-index component writes into workgroup vectors):
  - online attention, measured ×1.48 self / ×2.29 cross (−352 ms per 33-frame DiT forward);
  - i8a8 linear, measured ×1.32–1.39 against the Ampere table and ×1.25–1.28 against the Blackwell table (about −470
    to −530 ms per forward).
- **The remaining small ops, the VAE, the generic row kernels, other families' matrix kernels and the host side** each
  have measured bit-identical candidates (§4–§9). On this GPU, the separately measured parts add up to:
  - one 33-frame DiT forward 3,230 → about 2,160–2,220 ms (−31 to −33%);
  - one 121-frame DiT forward about −6.3 s (−30 to −33%);
  - the 33-frame VAE stage about −30 s on Node.
  No combined branch has been measured yet (§10).
- **The 5070 Ti transfer is the open question.** The pre-F-1 Chrome record bounds an estimate of −18 to −30% of a 326-s
  request (§10). A runbook with MUST-compliant pages, verified end to end in Chromium here, settles it in one sitting:
  `outputs/diag/perf-recon-2026-10-24/runbook/`.
- Numbers that change are kept apart. These are opt-in or need a ruling:
  - routing s16 on Blackwell;
  - VAE tile 24;
  - int8 conv2d in the VAE;
  - xor-round rounding barriers on the default path;
  - sampler-level levers: a step cache, a CFG window, fewer steps (§9.4). They are the largest levers of all, about −16
    to −50% of a request (estimated from the literature), and need a visual A/B.
- **This machine has a sustained-load slow mode.** Tiled GEMM-class kernels run ×15–42 for about 14 s at a time (§9.3). It
  reverses the sustained gain of keeping every Session resident here. The 5070 Ti records show no trace of it.

## 2. Method

- **Harness.** Chromium 156.0.8078.47 runs from Nix under Xvfb, driven by a dependency-free CDP driver.
  - The playground dev server serves any worktree file through Vite's `/@fs/` prefix, so a scratch page imports the
    runtime unchanged.
  - The same page opens on the 5070 Ti through the existing SSH tunnel.
  - Harness and GPU protocol: `outputs/diag/perf-recon-2026-10-24/_harness/README.md`.
- **Structure.**
  - Round 1: 8 lenses, one agent each, then one adversarial verifier per lens and a completeness critic.
  - Round 2: 8 lenses (MUST-compliant forms, the shared i8a8 skeleton, the VAE conv2d and I2V encoder, LLM decode,
    other families, the weight supply path, 121 frames, the runbook), verified the same way.
  - Round 3: the runbook fixes, sampler-level levers, LLM PLE on the HF path, and the burst trap.
  - Verifiers re-ran every A/B they could inside one lock hold. Their corrected figures are the ones quoted here.
- **Comparisons.** Every comparison is interleaved inside one GPU lock hold, shuffled per round, with round 0 dropped,
  and reports median and min. Correctness of every variant is checked word for word against production before its speed
  counts. "Bit-identical" below means 0 differing u32 words on Vulkan/NVIDIA (Node, and Chromium wherever a
  page was run; the TTS-family items are Node only). D3D12 and Metal are
  unchecked.
- **Measurement traps found on this machine.** Each one moved a reading by 4% to ×42:
  - the NVIDIA driver drops the memory clock from 9,501 to 5,001 MHz (P0 → P3) between sparse submits, halving bandwidth;
  - a fixed rotation order carries over up to 4% between neighbours;
  - run 1 of a graph is inflated;
  - 80–93 °C sustained runs inflate absolute times by 20–25% (one 121-frame forward heats the GPU from 57 to 93 °C within
    four forwards);
  - a sustained-load slow mode of this machine in which tiled GEMM-class kernels run ×15–42 for about 14 s at a time
    (§9.3);
  - a separate residency-probe pipeline reads different register pressure from the timed kernel;
  - repeated dispatches on the same buffers keep working sets under about 40 MB resident in the 5070 Ti's 48 MB L2, which
    does not show on the 3080 Ti's 6 MB L2.

## 3. Why online attention does not scale on Chrome/D3D12/Blackwell (F-2)

Attention route bench, ms per 1,000 query rows (production kernels):

| Shape | Node 3080 Ti s16 / online | Chromium-Linux 3080 Ti s16 / online | Chrome-Windows 5070 Ti s16 / online |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| self 7,920 (33f) | 4.077 / 3.578 | 4.095 / 3.611 | 2.97 / 3.87 |
| self 27,280 (121f) | 17.96 / 12.96 | 17.87 / 12.93 | 11.64 / 13.34 |
| cross 512 | 0.372 / 0.341 | 0.372 / 0.341 | 0.236 / 0.352 |

Per dispatch, 3080 Ti Node → 5070 Ti Chrome:
- s16: ①QK 5.80 → 3.75 ms, ② 3.00 → 2.13 ms, ③PV 7.35 → 5.88 ms;
- online: 28.34 → 30.65 ms, slower in absolute terms on a GPU with more SM × clock.

At equal s16 geometry the Blackwell gains are QK ×1.29 and PV ×1.10. So online loses about ×0.72–0.84 relative to the s16
kernels. On the 5070 Ti, wall and GPU timestamps agree within 1.4%, so a timestamp bias is excluded.

What this machine could establish:
- **Ruled out.** None of these varies between the two runtimes on this GPU:
  - Chrome's own Dawn, Tint, robustness and IPC (Chromium = Node within 1–3% on every variant);
  - the subgroup shuffle vs workgroup-memory reduction (equal);
  - the gather branches;
  - DXC inflating registers: DXIL translated through dxil-spirv into the same NVIDIA back end uses 111 registers.
- **Real Tint HLSL and DXIL.** The Dawn revision of Chrome 155 was built from source and compiled with Dawn's DXC
  arguments. Nothing in them is specific to the online kernel. The D3D12-only extras are:
  - `min()` clamps on every storage access: 49 in the online kernel; emulated on Vulkan they cost ×0.93–0.95;
  - `/Gis` precise floats;
  - `dot4add(a, b, 0)` followed by a separate add, which is also the SPIR-V shape.
- **Leading hypothesis H1: an occupancy cliff.** The production online kernel uses 112 registers (Vulkan form) and 118
  (clamped form). A 256-thread workgroup drops from 2 to 1 per SM above 128 registers. Pushing it over the cliff costs:
  - bit-identical register hogs: ×0.60–0.62;
  - net of the hog's own ALU (measured with ALU controls): ×0.65.
  A full cliff over-predicts the 5070 Ti deficit and the pure spill form (net ×0.88–0.90) under-predicts it, so H1 is
  the right family but not proven. Only the 5070 Ti page decides it, read through timing rules, not through the probe
  pipelines (§2).

- The one-factor split settled the round-1 conflict between the two attention lenses. Coalescing the Vᵀ fill matters only
  once the fill is unrolled: in production's loop form each load's latency is exposed. vec4 PV loads add +23% on top;
  vec4 QK loads add only +2–6%.
- Pre-laying Vᵀ in the kernel's key order on the producer side (ledger K-82's headroom note) pays only with an unrolled
  fill. It rewrites the same Vᵀ producer as the DiT's A3 (§6), and the two exclude each other.
- Registers on three compile paths (Vulkan form / clamped / DXC → dxil-spirv):
  - AC-1: 121 / 118 / 118, with no spill;
  - the variant without the PV split: 128 on every path, spilling 15–17 KB to shared memory on the DXC path;
  - AC-1 keeps 2 workgroups per SM even with 16 extra live values (a probe reading; the probes overstate register
    pressure, §2).
  These are proxies: the NVIDIA D3D12 driver's allocation is unobserved.
- AC-1's vec4 bindings of q, k, vs and o need 16-byte-aligned binding offsets and sizes, which the F-3 prologue (A1) and
  the BSHD store (A4) must keep. Its wg form grows to 35,328 B (production's wg form, 33,536 B, is already above D3D12's
  32 KiB).
- The route decision (online vs s16, both contract class Q) stays open on Blackwell. The two kernel rewrites, measured
  per 33f forward on the 3080 Ti:

  | Route | Today | Improved |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | online | 1,029 ms | 680 ms |
  | s16 | 1,112 ms | 813 ms |

  So online stays the right route here: ×1.20 at 33f and ×1.38 at 121f.
- The order on the 5070 Ti, estimated from the bench records:
  - today, s16 is ahead by about 1.48 s per 121f forward;
  - with both routes improved, the margin is anywhere from +126 to −87 ms per 33f forward, depending on which rewrite
    transfers.
  Choosing per adapter changes that adapter's output bits (ADR 0110, 0128), so it waits for the runbook.

## 5. The i8a8 linear GEMM and its shared skeleton

The DiT linear census at 33f is 310 dispatches per forward: 7,920×3,072×3,072 ×180, the FFN up and down ×30 each,
512-row cross linears ×61, and small ones. The production kernel reaches 31–42 TOPS against a measured dp4a ceiling of
64–74 TOPS (about 64 dp4a per SM per clock). Its limit is the K loop's per-tile overhead (fill + 2 barriers per 4 packs at
K 16, scalar 4-byte loads, a runtime pack loop), not dp4a issue.

| Candidate | What changes | Measured | Gain | Numerics |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SK-1 L1c (linear) | vec4 16-B global fill, K tile 32, pack loop unrolled in codegen, tile 128×64 r16×4 wg16×8, **scalar u32 shared tiles** (the compliant form) | per forward ×1.32–1.39 vs the Ampere table, ×1.25–1.28 vs the Blackwell table (Node and Chromium); qkvo ×1.23, up ×1.41, down ×1.43–1.61; 121f ×1.27 / ×1.41 / ×1.63–1.68 | 33f −470 to −530 ms (scaled to the in-pipeline linear total 1,884.5 ms); 121f −2.0 s | bit-identical (11 DiT shapes, 121f, 5 tail shapes) |
| SK-4 (conv3d w8a8) | the same on top of the VAE geometry fix (§7) | ×1.14 next / ×1.21 first chunk vs that fix | −3.5 s per 33f VAE stage (tile 16) | bit-identical |
| SK-5 (small M) | a second geometry for M below about 1,000 | avoids L1c's ×0.66–0.93 on small shapes | −2.3 ms per Wan forward; protects other families | bit-identical |
| OF-2 (M ≤ 4) | K-split GEMV with an exact i32 tree and the production epilogue | ×2.0–8.1 on Irodori's M = 1 adaLN linears | Irodori −306 ms GPU per utterance (−10%); anima about −50 ms | bit-identical |

- **The vec4 shared-tile form of round 1 breaks `gemm.ts:823`, and the compliant form is as fast.** Compliant: four
  static-component stores into scalar tiles. Round-1 form: `sa[w >> 2][r & 3] = v`, measured 1.4% slower than compliant.
- **The skeleton pieces are shared.** The fill fragments and `i8a8InnerProductLoop` are used by linear (and w4a8), the s16
  attention GEMMs and conv3d w8a8. Making the new options optional geometry fields keeps the default profile's WGSL
  byte-identical (ADR 0115 decision 5); 110 generator outputs were checked. Two MUSTs need attention:
  - `linear-i8a8.ts:298` requires i8 and w4a8 to share `fillTilesA`;
  - the ③PV prologue's row_max MUST (`attention-i8a8.ts:562`). The ③PV vec4 fill also needs N % 16 = 0: the 832×480
    shapes of Wan2.1 (81f) and Wan2.2 (33f) fall back to the word fill, whose gain is unmeasured. It is now covered by a per-row-m check; the original test
    data set row_max = 0 on every row and could not see an indexing error.
- **The geometry profile holds one i8a8 geometry for every M** (`geometry-profile.ts:100-104`). Adopting L1c alone would
  regress Irodori by an estimated +90 ms per utterance (measured ×0.90–0.93 at M = 185 and M = 1). So L1c,
  an M-bucketed i8a8 field and OF-2 are one design decision (ADR 0115/0116, ❌ K-3 revisited with a new fact).
- **The 5070 Ti gain is the uncertain part.** The same production kernel already runs at about 64 TOPS there. If
  Blackwell keeps 64 dp4a per SM per clock, that is about 69% of its ceiling. Estimated range: −70 to −230 ms per forward.
- Dropped:
  - unrolling only the 4-step pack loop: ×1.00 (the f32 effect does not carry over);
  - ping-pong double buffering: no gain;
  - register prefetch: occupancy 2 → 1, ×0.63–0.71;
  - the Blackwell-table geometry on Ampere: ×1.09 but regresses cross and small shapes, so superseded.

- The compliant set A1–C3 is about −160 ms per 33f forward (5%) and about −690 ms per 121f forward. With E1 (net of its 0.67 ms
  overlap with C1) and F1 (an upper bound) it is about −183 ms at 33f. A4, C1 and E1 are trace estimates, not spikes. The
  decision line of K-7 (−1% of the total GPU) is passed by F-3 (A1, A2, A4: −69 ms, 2.1%) and F-4 alone; F-8 (A3) is
  under it at 33f but −159 ms at 121f; F-6 sits on the line, and F-7 is under it alone.
- A1–A3 take over the attention node's own quantize dispatches; the `buildAttentionOnline` MUST then requires both a8
  routes (online and s16) to switch together.
- **Rounding barriers.** Production's default-path fusions stage rounding through u32 workgroup memory, as the MUSTs in
  `rope.ts` and `adaln-norm.ts` require. ADR 0099's xor-round is opt-in without a backend guarantee. The xor forms gain

- **FMA contraction is load-bearing.** Production `rms_norm` matches only because the driver contracts `acc + v*v` into an

- Dropped: CFG as one batch-2 forward. It measured ±2%: the GEMMs are already large, so batching gains nothing (§9.4 for
  the sampler-level alternative).

## 8. Other families and the generic kernels

- **The f32 GEMM skeleton** (`gemm.ts`) serves linear, matmul, bmm, the conv igemm, fused f32 attention and the
  states-form attention. It reaches 7–11 TFLOP/s against a measured 36 TFLOP/s FMA ceiling. The compliant rewrite C1a
  combines three changes, keeps the per-output reduction order and is bit-identical at kernel level (the gemma4 text sha
  was checked with it; the other model shas only with round 1's non-compliant form):
  - a k-major A tile in a scalar array;
  - contiguous B column quads per thread;
  - the 16-step kk loop emitted unrolled, the largest factor: the NVIDIA Vulkan compiler does not unroll Tint's loop.

  Results:
  - ×1.42–1.46 on r8×8 shapes;
  - gemma4 E2B warm TTFT ×1.21.

  Round 1's form also replaced the B-tile switch with `sb[i][wsl] = v` (×1.10–1.27 more). That breaks `gemm.ts:823`
  and is dropped. The rewrite moves the default WGSL bytes of every f32 GEMM op (`gemm.ts:39` MUST), so it needs a key
  bump and snapshot regeneration. An intermittent NVVM pipeline-compile failure was seen three times in long
  multi-pipeline runs; it must be explained before an unrolled skeleton ships.
- **Conv igemm.** A strength-reduced im2col decode adds ×1.30 for conv2d (lower bound measured with constants) — CF-1 is
  its Wan form.
- **deform_conv2d as an implicit GEMM (K-63).** Bit-identical, ×4–49 per dispatch. BiRefNet-HR 1024 GPU 1,396 → 365 ms,
  wall ×3.4.
- **Row kernels.** quantize_rows, rms_norm, layer_norm and the adaLN token body run at 55–82% of the measured 840 GB/s
  copy roofline, because they re-read the row from DRAM. Register-resident forms with the same lane assignment and tree
  are bit-identical and reach 96–103%:
  - M-1 quantize (about −27 ms per Wan forward on Node, mostly inside B1);
  - M-2 one row per subgroup at D 128;
  - M-3 norms.
  The 5070 Ti already runs these at 80–86% of its bandwidth (48 MB L2), so the gain there is discounted to roughly
  30–50%.
- **Already at the roofline.** Elementwise ops (92–101%) and permutes (96–97%; P-5's rejection holds). After F-1 the
  remaining tiny dispatches cost about 0.5 ms per forward: a dispatch inside a pass costs about 1.0–1.3 µs.
- **sbv2** is host-bound: 646–717 ms of wall for 105.7 ms of GPU per sentence. Its three Sessions are built and
  disposed per generate:
  - keeping them (H-4) gives 104–107 ms from the third generate on (×6.7);
  - grid-aware conv1d tiles (OF-4, K-64) give about −35 ms GPU (−33%).
- **Irodori** (2,895 ms GPU per utterance):
  - OF-2 GEMV −306 ms;
  - a phase-tiled conv_transpose1d (OF-3, the step after K-10) −244 ms, ×3.6–4.5 per layer.
- **anima** (6,830 ms GPU per image):
  - L1c −415 ms, needs M-bucketing;
  - keeping the text stages resident (H-36) about −2.1 s of wall beyond ADR 0112's DiT-only opt-in, per isolated
    generate. In sustained gap-free use on this machine it is slower instead (§9.3).
- Not covered: gru-scan and cumsum. They are probably small (sbv2's conv1d is 64% of its GPU).

## 9. LLM decode and the host side

### 9.1 gemma4 E2B decode (i4-fast)

A token costs 10.1–10.4 ms on Node, of which the GPU is busy 6.2 ms (894 dispatches in one submit). LD-1 and LD-3 are
bit-identical (the readback hash f3988176c4e2e439 is equal across arms and between Node and Chromium); LD-4 is an upper
bound from the greedy arm with no kernel built, so its bit identity is unchecked.

- **LD-1: chunked submit (H-29 revived).** Today the whole token goes out as one submit, so the GPU waits for the JS
  encode (1.48 ms) and Dawn's command recording (0.93 ms). A cap of 128 dispatches per submit gives:
  - Node 10.1 → 7.4 ms/token (×1.35);
  - Chromium 10.0 → 8.0 ms (×1.24);
  - QAT E2B ×1.31, QAT E4B ×1.20.
  - Not additive with H-27 (run-ahead hides the same host time), and it shrinks MTP's win on repeat text (×1.9 at cap
    1024 → ×1.15–1.43 at cap 128).
- **LD-3: stage the activation in workgroup memory in the l32 parallel GEMV.** This explains round 1's 300 GB/s plateau:
  the activation loads, not the weights, limit it (research 2026-09-19 §14 saw the same on QAT). Down 1536×12288:
  42 → 26 µs. Token GPU 6.4 → 5.9 ms. It must be a separate keyed
  variant (`linear-gemv.ts:521` forbids changing the existing generated text).
- **LD-4: GPU top-k for the default sampler (H-32).** Measured upper bound −0.44 ms (Node) / −0.25 ms (Chromium); the
  first readings were inflated 2–3× by the measurement's own readback hashing and copies.
- **LD-2: host PLE row misses on local-directory sources re-read and re-hash a whole 33.5 MB block.** This costs 33–57 ms
  per token and seconds of TTFT. It is already filed (backlog "later", 2026-09-24; L-16/L-17). Round 3 measured the
  other sources:
  - **The product browser path (Chromium + HF + CacheStorage) does not have it.** A row miss is a 0.20 ms Blob slice:
    decode 10.2–11.3 ms/token with every row missing, and the all-miss vs all-hit difference is 0.35 ms/token.
  - **Node + HF (a product path) is slow for another reason (L-14).** The scan reader holds one part, so every row miss
    reads a whole 33.5 MB part: decode 25.4 vs 10.1 ms/token.
- **The PLE row cache (H-22) stops admitting rows after the first prompt with 32 or more distinct ids.** The traced rule
  admits a row only if both its values and its scales came from row reads. The first such prompt loads scales block 0
  (91% of the vocabulary) whole, and from then on every row is refused. Once a values block is whole too, the default
  budget (2 × the largest block) leaves 8,120 B, less than one 9,100 B row.
  - The fix copies the row out of a resident block and keeps 256 rows of headroom (2.33 MB, the maximum payload ADR 0085
    already states). Measured on Node + HF (fresh / extract / extract): decode medians 20.4–21.2 → 10.3–10.6 ms/token.
    The extract figures are an upper bound, because its decode ids repeat prompt ids (fresh mean 16.8 → 13.6). A
    copy-only fix admits nothing once a values block is resident too. The fix's TTFT is unmeasured.
  - The product browser path gains at most about 0.35 ms/token.
  - L-15 (scales resident at load) must not land without this fix: it would put every source in the refused state from
    the first token.
- **LD-5 (re-tune the MTP gate): refuted.** The gate measures its inputs at run time, and the free-text loss is its
  designed probe (about 300 ms per sequence).

### 9.2 Session builds and the weight supply (browser)

A Wan2.2 request re-reads 11.8 GB from CacheStorage. The candidates below are bit-identical. HB-1 and SP-1 add (SP-1 is
measured beyond HB-1). SP-2 + SP-3 and SP-2 + SP-4 are alternatives, each measured beyond HB-1 + SP-1; the best stack,
HB-1 + SP-1 + SP-2/SP-4, takes the builds from 12.6 to about 4.2 s per request.

| Step | Mechanism | Chromium-Linux per request | 5070 Ti per request (estimate) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| HB-1 | await each initializer's error-scope pair at the part fence, not per item (`session-build.ts:969-986`) — today supply and upload run strictly in series, and SessionBuildStats does not see the waits | −3.0 to −3.2 s | −1.2 to −1.7 s |
| SP-1 | read 2 blocks ahead across part boundaries (per part only −0.6 s) | −1.0 s | −0.5 to −1.0 s |
| SP-2 + SP-3 | reused MAP_WRITE staging ring (upload 2.1 → 7–8.7 GB/s) + 4-worker CacheStorage reads | −2.2 s | unknown (Windows page faults) |
| SP-2 + SP-4 | OPFS sync-handle reads into reused buffers instead of SP-3 | about −4.1 s | at most −4.6 s with SP-1 |

The read ceiling of every API that returns a fresh ArrayBuffer per block (2.6–3.1 GB/s) is first-touch page faults:
allocating without touching runs at 54–83 GB/s, touching every page at 2.9–3.0 GB/s. The disk and IPC are not the limit.
No sha256 runs on the browser path.

Other host items:
- HB-2: process each VAE tile while the GPU decodes the next one: about −1.35 s at 33f and −5.7 s at 121f on the 5070 Ti
  (estimated: the CPU tail measured here, the overlap estimated), and 0.7 / 2.7 GB less host RAM.
- HB-3 (K-79): vae_decoder_first borrows all of vae_decoder_next's weights. All 109 of them are byte-identical: about
  −0.98 s per request on the 5070 Ti (from its record's build stats), −0.5 GiB VRAM, −0.54 GB of download. A
  borrower-side layout assertion is advised (`resolveSharedWeights` does not compare layouts).
- HB-4: tap-major reorder with u32 writes, −0.75 s.
- HB-6 (H-34): u32-pair splitmix64, 5.7× faster, about −0.15 s. The five copies are deliberate, so it is applied per copy.
- HB-7: an umT5 embedding cache, −4 s on a re-roll only.
- HB-8: DiT Session residency, about −2.7 to −3.0 s from the second request on after HB-1. It needs a VRAM ruling.
- The steady denoise loop is already GPU-bound: 63 ms of host per 5.27-s step (H-37's rejection holds).
- Pipeline compile is negligible on Vulkan. D3D12 cold compile is in the runbook.

### 9.3 A sustained-load slow mode on this machine

The other-families verifier saw 16× slow DiT steps almost only when the host gaps were removed. Round 3 reproduced it
without anima and characterised it:
- **What slows.** Tiled GEMM-class kernels slow ×15–42 for whole submits: the production i8a8 GEMM ×18–19, the s16 attention
  GEMMs, and anima's VAE tiles ×33 (its f32 igemm conv kernels about ×45–50, estimated). Other kernels in the same submits stay ×0.97–1.01: a streaming copy, a
  workgroup-memory loop, an L2-reuse loop, dp4a and FMA ALU loops, and the elementwise, quantize and norm kernels.
- **The episodes.** Each episode lasts 5–8 submits (about 14 s of slow GPU time). With 128 GEMMs per submit they recur every
  18–30 s, and 46–71% of GPU time is slow.
- **The state during an episode.** The memory controller reads 96–100% busy (normally 8–35%). SM and memory clocks stay at
  P0, power at 290–345 W, and no thermal or power-brake counter fires.
- **Two triggers:** sustained gap-free P0 load (recurring episodes), and the wake from P3 after an idle gap (one episode,
  typically on the first DiT step after a Session build).
- **Ruled out:** heat, the clock state, time slicing with another context, VRAM placement and working-set size (a single
  fixed 4 MiB weight enters it as often as 64 rotating weights), and Node (Chromium reproduces it).
- **Machine facts.** A 4K monitor is attached to the 3080 Ti, persistence mode is off and the addressing mode is HMM. The
  mechanism is open; isolating it needs host access (display detached, persistence on).
- **It attaches to the pipeline object** (round 4, measured). During an episode the affected pipeline stays slow even on
  its own buffers and in a 2-dispatch submit (15.5–15.7 ms). A fresh pipeline built from the same WGSL with a comment
  added runs at normal speed on the affected pipeline's buffers (0.75–0.98 ms). A sentinel on a second device never
  slowed. The runbook's guard therefore compares each timed pipeline with such a fresh twin.
- **Not on the 5070 Ti records.** Steps 2–50 run at max/median ≤ 1.02 and VAE tiles at 1.77–1.81 s, about 800 s of sustained
  DiT with no episode. The 121f forwards here show heat but no episode.

Consequences:
- The sustained gains of residency on this machine are not just unproven but reversed. Measured on anima, gap-free:
  - all Sessions resident: 22.1 s mean per image, 22 of 30 iterations slow;
  - per stage: 14.9 s.
- A smaller submit budget (25 ms) does not help: 7 of 10 slow. An idle gap of 1–3 s after each generate lowers the rate
  (2 of 30) but is a symptomatic workaround.
- Most kernel A/Bs were re-run by the verifiers in independent holds, and many on both runtimes (the other-families items
  and the cache zeroing on Node only); their ratios stand. Two runbook page cases were outlier-contaminated and were
  corrected or flagged.
  Absolute times of long gap-free runs on this machine need the burst-aware protocol (process-interleaved arms, per-run
  times, a GEMM canary). The runbook carries a canary page.

### 9.4 Sampler-level levers (literature and code scope; they change numbers)

CFG doubles the DiT cost: two forwards per step, about 263 of 326 s of a 5070 Ti request; one skipped forward saves about
2.6 s (0.8%). Upstream facts:
- TI2V-5B defaults: 50 UniPC steps, guide 5.0, shift 5.0.
- Neither the official code nor diffusers' Wan pipeline truncates CFG.
- Shift 5 packs the schedule at high noise: 25 of 50 steps have σ > 0.844, and only 8 have σ < 0.5. So "guidance off over
  the last X% of steps" reaches into the mid-noise band, not only the low-noise tail.

| ID | Lever | Request-level gain on the 5070 Ti | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SL-0 | diagnostics probe: per-step cos(cond, uncond) and the step-cache error estimate, logged on the unchanged path | none (bit-identical); decides SL-1 / SL-4 settings without a sweep | host cost 1.3–1.7 ms per step at 33f (measured) |
| SL-1 | EasyCache-style whole-model step cache (skip both forwards on a step, reuse x + Δ per branch) | upper bound −221 s; about −148 to −174 s (−45 to −53%) if the published TI2V-5B 720p ratio (2.3×) holds at 33f | literature: Wan2.1-1.3B 2.54×, VBench 80.49 vs 81.30; official EasyCache4Wan2.2 script |
| SL-2 | fewer steps (the existing `steps` knob) | 40 steps −53 s (−16%), 30 −105 s, 20–25 −132 to −158 s | arithmetic; on Wan2.1 a 20-of-50 cut is within 0.19 VBench of EasyCache at the same speed |
| SL-3 | CFG window (cond only outside a step-fraction window, as diffusers' modular guider does) | last 10 steps −26 s (−8%), last 20 −53 s (−16%), half −66 s (−20%) | literature on CogVideoX / SD-XL / images, none on Wan |
| SL-4 to SL-7 | adaptive guidance (switch on a cosine), block-residual caches (MagCache / TeaCache), FasterCache's CFG-Cache, CFG-Zero* zero-init | unknown to −68 s; CFG-Zero* −5 s | literature on other models; parked behind SL-0 / SL-1 |

- Rejected for karume: PAB, TaylorSeer and AdaCache (memory and per-block graph splits), guidance off at high noise, batch-2
  CFG, and distillation (ADR 0118).
- **These scale with the kernel work, they do not add to it.** Every seconds figure assumes today's 2.64-s forward, and
  the kernel candidates shrink the forward. Quote the levers as a fraction of DiT time; the ceiling is the DiT share,
  about 82% of a request.
- **Quality A/B design.** The A/B needs a matched-speed step-count arm (20–25 steps against SL-1), because SL-1's
  advantage on Wan2.1 is fidelity to the 50-step video, not proven better absolute quality. Use seeds 42–45 × 2–3
  prompts (the standing visual A/B rule).

## 11. Open questions and the answer check

- **The runbook.** `outputs/diag/perf-recon-2026-10-24/runbook/` (README.md, STEPS.ja.md, index.html) is one sitting.
  Step 01 comes first: it re-runs the two playground scenarios with GPU timing. That gives the post-F-1 baseline, and its
  D3D12 hash gate checks the published records' rgbaSha256 for the 17-frame and 33-frame scenarios. Then come attention
  (H1, the AC-1 transfer, the route), linear and the dp4a ceiling, small ops, VAE, host, and optionally LLM decode and
  other families. Each step saves a fixed-name JSON (and an OPFS copy after every part). Round 3 applied the critics'
  fixes: timing-based reading rules, a per-row row_max in the ③PV check, a 121f part, burst flags, a ratio-of-ratios
  route reading, buffer rotation above 2.5 × 48 MB, and a scope-aware `gemm.ts:823` scanner.
  - Its verifier still found that this machine's slow mode (§9.3) silently corrupted whole cases in 2 of 3 full page
    runs.
  - Rounds 4–5 added a slow-mode guard. It compares every timed pipeline with a fresh twin at the end of its case and
    re-runs the case on a ×2 gap: on the originals once, then on twins. They also added a canary preflight (step 00b),
    and an uncaptured WebGPU error now turns a part into a recorded failure.
  - The code was then frozen (63 sha256 recorded), and the whole sitting ran on it in Chromium here: 0 uncaptured
    errors, the reference values reproduced, and the verifier judged it ready to hand over.
  - Known limits (in its README): a moderate whole-case state (×1.3–2), or one that also covers the twins, is accepted.
    On a machine where step 00b reports the slow mode, steps 02, 03 and 05 run twice and only agreeing ratios are read.
    Under D3D12's 32 KiB limit, two wg-form variants are rejected and leave an unpopped error scope for the rest of
    part 02b.
