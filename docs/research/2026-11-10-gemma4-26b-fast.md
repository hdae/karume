# Gemma 4 26B A4B: where a decode forward goes, and its parallel GEMV rows (2026-11-10)

A point-in-time measurement record. It explains why 26B's only preset was the reference order, gives the sweep behind its
rows in the parallel GEMV table, and lists what is left of a forward. Scripts and logs:
`outputs/diag/forward-cost-2026-11-10/`.

## 1. Summary

- Asking for the parallel forms changed nothing on 26B's dense linears: the parallel GEMV applies only to the shapes in
  its measured table, and no row had 26B's width (2816). The RMS→add fusion did not list the width either. So 26B ran
  its 236 dense linears on the sequential GEMV, about half of a decode forward.
- With ten rows and the width added, one decode forward takes 17.5–17.9 ms in place of 24.6–25.7 ms on the RTX 3080 Ti
  (×0.70), with the same tokens on the prompts measured. 26B gains the `i4-gemvpar` and `i4-fast` presets.
- In Chrome on the RTX 5070 Ti `i4-fast` decodes at 80.9 tokens per second with every expert resident (f16 KV) and
  at 30.1 under the expert cache with self-speculation (22.8 with `i4`), with the same tokens (§6).
- What is left of a forward: the small kernels (norms, elementwise; about 940 dispatches) 6.3 ms, the dense linears
  5.6 ms, the routed experts 2.9 ms, attention 0.9 ms.

## 2. Setup

The RTX 3080 Ti (12 GB, Node + Dawn) cannot hold 26B resident, so every run here decodes under the exact expert cache
(ADR [0146](../decisions/0146-exact-expert-cache.md)): 64 slots per layer, every expert in the host tier, a missed run
run again, no self-speculation. One process per arm, greedy, three prompts of 96 new tokens after a warm-up. **A
forward** below is the wall time of a one-row run without a miss (median of 285); it does not depend on the misses.
**Decode** is the wall per token of the whole loop, re-runs and loads included. Per-kernel GPU time comes from
timestamp queries (one pass per dispatch), which slows the wall but not the kernels' own times: the per-kernel sum of
the reference arm (25.5 ms) matches its untimed forward (24.6–25.7 ms). The GPU ran at 54–86 °C; the arms of §4 and §5
were cooled to 60 °C before each start.

## 5. The presets with the rows in the tree

Two rounds, the options each preset declares. All arms generated the same tokens.

| Preset | Forward (ms) | Decode under the cache (ms per token) |
| ------ | -----------: | ------------------------------------: |
| `i4` | 24.6 / 25.7 | 60.8 / 63.8 |
| `i4-gemvpar` | 17.7 / 17.9 | 46.4 / 46.2 |
| `i4-fast` | 17.6 / 17.5 | 46.0 / 45.8 |

Per-kernel GPU time of a decode run, `i4-fast`:

| Kernels | Dispatches | ms |
| ------- | ---------: | --: |
| dense linears (parallel GEMV) | 236 | 5.6 |
| small kernels (90 RMS→add pairs fused) | 938 | 6.3 |
| routed experts (`expert_linear`, pooled, unchanged) | 60 | 2.9 |
| attention (parallel, fused) | 60 | 0.9 |
| total | 1,294 | 15.7 |

- The fusion at width 2816 removes 90 dispatches and about 0.5 ms of GPU time; in wall time `i4-fast` is 0.1–0.4 ms
  ahead of `i4-gemvpar` on this card.
- The tokens matched `i4` on these three prompts. The parallel forms change the rounding, so that is an observation,
  not a guarantee.
- Under the expert cache the reference of exactness is unchanged: a run of the same row count takes the same kernels
  with every expert resident and with the cache, and `expert_linear` keeps its sequential order under every option. The
  parallel GEMV applies to runs of 1 to 8 rows, which covers the cache's chunks at up to 71 slots and the verify of
  self-speculation at up to depth 7.

## 6. Chrome on the RTX 5070 Ti

The maintainer ran the chat of [research 2026-11-08](2026-11-08-gemma4-26b-moe-cache.md) §6.5 (the same three turns,
temperature 1, top-k 64, top-p 0.95, seed 0, 360 decode tokens) with `i4-fast` from the local mirror. Records
`gemma4_2026-11-10T10-31-57.json` to `…T10-41-06.json`; the `i4` rows are the earlier records of the same chat.

| Preset | Experts | Full-layer KV | Decode tokens per s |
| ------ | ------- | ------------- | ------------------: |
| `i4` | resident | f32 | 48.8 |
| `i4-fast` | resident | f32 | 15.7, then 3.2 (stopped by hand in the third turn) |
| `i4` | resident | f16 | 49.1 |
| `i4-fast` | resident | f16 | 80.9 |
| `i4` | fitted slots, depth 4, host tier | f32 | 22.8 |
| `i4-fast` | fitted slots, depth 4, host tier | f32 | 30.1 |
| `i4-fast` | fitted slots, depth 4, host tier | f16 | 29.9 |

- **Every completed run generated the same tokens** in each turn — both presets, both KV storages, resident and
  cached — and the three cached runs counted the same cycles (112 cycles, 345 drafts confirmed of 440).
- **Under the cache the preset gains ×1.32** (33.2 against 43.9 ms per decode token). The loads are unchanged (about
  4.3–4.6 s over the chat), so the gain is in the runs.
- **Resident, 12.4 ms per token** (80.9 tokens per second), against 20.4 with `i4` (the f16 baseline below).
- **The resident f32 run fell out of dedicated memory**: 15.7 tokens per second in the first turn and 3.2 after it,
  with prefill at 15–32 tokens per second in place of 180–280. The maintainer reports the same slowdown on earlier
  resident runs from time to time. `fullStateStorage: "f16"` (ADR
  [0143](../decisions/0143-f16-full-state-storage.md)) removed it here; the cached runs do not depend on it.
- The `i4` run with the f16 KV (`gemma4_2026-11-10T11-07-22.json`, recorded after the others) matches the f32 one, so
  that baseline was not short of memory: the preset's resident gain is ×1.65.

- The fitted allocation bounds the depth at 4 (its smallest layer holds 40 experts, so a run has at most 5 rows).
- Every arm of a temperature generated the same tokens.
- Depths 2 to 4 are within the noise of each other (the repeated arm differs by up to 2.9 ms), depth 1 gains nothing
  and depths above 4 give the gain back: more drafts are thrown away (drafts run per confirmed draft: 1.14 at depth 2,
  1.31 at depth 4, 1.47 at depth 7 at temperature 0). The cheaper forward did not move the best depth; depth 4, the
  value of the Chrome runs, stays a reasonable choice.
- On this card self-speculation at depths 2 to 4 takes ×0.82–0.97 of the decode time without it, most arms near ×0.9;
  in Chrome on the RTX 5070 Ti the same change was ×0.84 with `i4` (research 2026-11-08 §6.5).

## 8. The small kernels of a layer

A read of the product graph against the kernel keys of a decode run with `i4-fast` (no measurement). One layer is 72
graph nodes and about 43 dispatches; 31 of them are small kernels (938 per forward with the final norm and the
embeddings), each 6–8 µs on the RTX 3080 Ti whatever its size.

| Part of a layer | Small dispatches now | As one kernel each | Saved | What it needs |
| --------------- | -------------------: | -----------------: | ----: | ------------- |
| dense MLP: `gelu_tanh(gate) · up` | 2 | 1 | 1 | an elementwise chain, one output |
| experts: two slices of `gate_up`, `gelu_tanh`, `mul` | 4 | 1 | 3 | an elementwise chain over strided views, one output |
| router input: `rms_norm`, `mul` by a constant | 2 | 1 | 1 | a scale after the norm, one output |
| router weights: `sum`, `div`, `embedding` (per-expert scale), `mul` | 4 | 1 | 3 | one output from the top-k values and ids |
| experts' output: `mul` by the weights, `sum` over the 8 picks | 2 | 1 | 1 | a weighted sum, one output |
| q and k: `rms_norm` then RoPE (half form), twice | 4 | 2 | 2 | the half-form sibling of the existing `rmsNormRope` rule |
| layer output: `mul` by the layer scalar after the fused norm and add | 2 | 1 | 1 | a scale folded into `rms_norm_add` |
| `softmax` then `topk` | 2 | 1 | 1 | a fused step with two outputs |
| three norms of one input (dense MLP, router, experts) | 3 | 1 | 2 | a fused step with three outputs; the reduction is computed once |
| the K and V appends | 2 | 1 | 1 | a fused step that writes state |

- The first seven rows are single-output and write no state: 12 dispatches per layer, 360 per forward. The last three
  need what the fusion planner forbids today (`windowIsSingleOutput`, `windowTouchesState`): 4 more per layer, 120 per
  forward.
- At 6.7 µs per small dispatch that is about 2.4 ms of the 15.7 ms forward on the RTX 3080 Ti for the first group and
  about 3.2 ms with the second (an estimate from the per-kernel times, which are taken with one pass per dispatch).
- Every row keeps each output's arithmetic and order, so each is a candidate for a bit-identical fusion; whether the
  compiler keeps the multiply-adds apart is an exact-match test per rule, as for `rms_norm_add`.
- These are instances of one generic rule (an elementwise chain with one output) plus three or four specific ones, so
  they belong with the generated fusion pass that is being designed rather than with ten hand-written rules for one
  model. Nothing here was built.
